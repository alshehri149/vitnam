The route

Tap any leg to open it. The shape is simple — a day in Hanoi off the red-eye, two nights up in Sapa, three down on the coast at Da Nang, then one night back in Hanoi before the flight home. Hotels and per-day activities are still to be filled in; the timing below is what holds it together.

The day, by the clock

You land at 07:15 and this whole day is yours in Hanoi — nothing ambitious after a red-eye. Bags down, a nap if you need one, food, and a slow first look at the city before an early start for Sapa tomorrow.

  • 07:15 — land at Noi Bai. Immigration, bags, and a SIM card at arrivals. Don't assume it's quick.
  • 09:15 — out of the airport. Kerbside with the bags, into a taxi or Grab for the Old Quarter.
  • 10:15 — at the hotel. Drop the bags. If the room is ready, sleep for a couple of hours; if not, leave the bags at the desk and head out.
  • Afternoon — Hoan Kiem Lake and a first lap of the Old Quarter to reset your body clock, with the first shopping window (Uniqlo is five minutes off the lake — see the shopping section).
  • Evening — wander until night. Ta Hien street for the crowds and a sugarcane juice, or Train Street if the schedule lines up. A halal dinner around Al Noor, then an early night before the 06:00 start.
Move · Hanoi → Sapa · tomorrow 06:00Leave Hanoi at 06:00 Wednesday by expressway limousine van, into Sapa around 12:00 — about six hours with a comfort stop. Book the seats before you fly. Sleep tonight; there is no need for the old overnight train on this plan.

The days

Up at 1,600 m for two nights. Late August is peak green, right before the September gold. Two nights is enough for the headline things if you pick rather than try to do everything — the afternoon you arrive, one full day, then out early.

  • Wed 26 — leave Hanoi 06:00, into Sapa around 12:00. Check in, lunch, then an easy afternoon: the Cat Cat village loop or a short valley walk to shake off the drive. Nothing hard on arrival day.
  • Thu 27 — the full day. Either Fansipan by cable car (be at the station by 08:00, before cloud closes in) or the Lao Chai–Ta Van trek through the terraces, 5–6 h and moderate. Hire a H'mong guide from the village, not a town agency.
  • Fri 28 — out early. Pack the night before and leave Sapa at 06:00 sharp; the midday flight from Hanoi depends on it. No slow morning here.
  • Bac Ha market doesn't fall in your window — it runs Sundays only, and your Sunday is on the coast. Skip it without regret.

The days

The change of pace on this trip: down from the mountains to a long sweep of beach on the central coast, with a run of the country's best day trips within an hour. Three nights, and the longest single stay — this is where the group slows down.

  • Fri 28 — land 16:00, hotel 17:00. Off the Sapa road at Noi Bai around 12:00, fly early afternoon, into Da Nang by 16:00. Ease in: My Khe beach for a swim, or the Han riverfront and the Dragon Bridge in the evening.
  • Sat 29 & Sun 30 — Da Nang and around. The Marble Mountains and Son Tra peninsula are on the doorstep; Ba Na Hills and the Golden Bridge are a half day inland; and Hoi An — the lantern-lit old town — is 45 minutes south and worth an evening on its own. Beach mornings, sights before the afternoon heat.
  • Keep one slot loose for weather and for how tired the group still is after Sapa. These are suggestions, to be firmed up later.

Monday afternoon, then out

Back in Hanoi by midday with a full afternoon and evening — a comfortable buffer before an early departure, and the second shopping window. You leave for the airport at 04:00, so everything you still want to do happens on the Monday, not the morning of the 1st.

  • Mon 11:00 — into Hanoi off the flight from Da Nang.
  • Mon 12:00 — check in. Then the rest of the day is Hanoi: lunch, the second shopping window, collect any tailoring you ordered on the way in, a last lap of the Old Quarter.
  • Mon evening — a last halal dinner, then pack tonight and book the airport car at reception for 04:00. Don't leave the packing to the morning.
  • Tue 1 Sep — check out and leave for the airport at 04:00. Empty roads make Noi Bai about 45 minutes, so you're airside with hours to spare — comfortable margin on the one flight you cannot miss.

Shopping in Hanoi

You have two Hanoi windows, both roomier than the old plan allowed: the afternoon and evening of Tuesday 25th on the way in, and the afternoon and evening of Monday 31st on the way out. The 1st gives you nothing — you leave for the airport at 04:00. Order anything tailored on the Tuesday so it's ready to collect Monday, and leave the bulk of the buying to Monday when your bags are already heading home.

1 Ba Trieu · Hoan Kiem

Uniqlo Hoan Kiem

Three floors in the Hanoi Tourist Building on the lake's south-east corner, five minutes from the Old Quarter and open 10:00–22:00 daily. Vietnam is where a lot of this is cut and sewn, and the local pricing reflects it — the same AIRism and linen you would buy at home, materially cheaper. Both of your windows fall inside opening hours, and Monday night it is the only serious shop still open at 21:00.

Two streets of silk, tailoring and embroidery running west off the lake. Same-day alterations are routine and a shirt can be made in 24–48 hours — which only works if you order on the Tuesday and collect on the Monday. Bring a garment you already like the fit of. Northern edge of the Old Quarter

Dong Xuan market

Three floors of wholesale everything — clothing, fabric, luggage, household goods. Cheapest per item and the least comfortable shopping of the three; go for volume and gifts rather than anything you need to try on. Closes around 19:00, so it is an early-evening stop on the Monday or a Tuesday afternoon detour.

Closes ≈19:00 · bargain hard

Sizing

Vietnamese sizing runs small by European or Gulf standards — often a full size, sometimes two. Try things on rather than trusting the label, and note that Uniqlo stocks international sizing while the markets do not.

Bag maths

Leave real space, or buy a cheap holdall at Dong Xuan for the way home. Check your airline's cabin allowance before Monday evening — the shopping happens roughly ten hours before you fly, which is too late to discover you are over.

Bargaining

Fixed prices at Uniqlo and the malls. Everywhere else, open at about half and settle near two-thirds. Walking away is a normal part of the conversation, not an insult.

If you want a mall

Vincom Center Pham Ngoc Thach and Trang Tien Plaza are both open late and both a short taxi from the lake. Worth it only if the Old Quarter has not produced what you wanted — the lake stores are closer to where you sleep.

Al Noor Mosque

Built by Bombay traders in 1885 and still the only mosque in northern Vietnam. Jumu'ah does not fall on your Hanoi days — the one Friday of the trip, 28 August, you're on the road down from Sapa and then flying to Da Nang. Come for a weekday prayer and lunch instead: halal kitchens cluster around the gate, and there's a halal-food board visible in this photo. Da Nang also has a mosque if you want Friday prayer on the coast.

On the shortlist

Crew notes

Booking rhythm

Book first: the two domestic flights (Hanoi–Da Nang on the 28th, Da Nang–Hanoi on the 31st) and the Sapa van, since the tight connection depends on the flight times. Hotels are all still TBD — pick them next, and don't leave the two Hanoi nights or the Sapa nights late for a late-August window.

The tight connection

Friday the 28th is the one day a delay hurts: Sapa down to Noi Bai is six hours by road, feeding a same-day flight to Da Nang. Take the mid-afternoon departure rather than the earliest, leave Sapa at 06:00, and keep no side stops on that drive.

Eating halal on this route

Hanoi is easy — certified kitchens cluster around Al Noor mosque. Sapa town has two reliable halal restaurants; out in the valley, arrange it with your hotel. Da Nang and Hoi An have a real halal scene and a mosque, to be confirmed before the trip. Say "khong thit lon" — no pork — and expect to repeat it.

Cash

Homestays, village guides and small stops are frequently cash-only, especially in Sapa. Draw dong in Hanoi before heading up — mountain ATMs are unreliable.

Packing that matters

Broken-in shoes with grip; the Sapa terraces turn to clay when wet. A rain shell, not an umbrella. One warm layer for Sapa nights and Fansipan — then swimwear and light clothes for three days on the Da Nang coast.

What the flight times give you

Both ends are generous on this plan. A 07:15 landing hands you a whole first day in Hanoi; coming back from Da Nang on the Monday puts a relaxed final evening and the second shopping window before the 04:00 airport run. Nothing is squeezed at the edges.

Splitting up is fine

Fansipan cable car versus a full trek day is the natural fork in Sapa. In Da Nang it's beach days versus the inland day trips. Set a meet-back time and let people choose.

Guides

In Sapa, hire from the village, not a town agency. In Da Nang you don't need a guide — a car and driver for a day covers the Marble Mountains, Son Tra and Hoi An comfortably.

The bigger swap

If the group wants wilder mountains and doesn't mind rough roads, trade Sapa for the Ha Giang Loop — 3–4 days, better scenery, far fewer people. It's a much larger time cost, though, and would eat into the three coastal days at Da Nang.

Reading the weather

Two different climates on this trip. The north — Hanoi and Sapa — is in rainy season in late August: clear or hazy mornings, humidity building, then a heavy burst mid-to-late afternoon lasting 30 to 90 minutes, with evenings often drying out. Schedule the demanding Sapa things before noon. The central coast at Da Nang is usually hotter and drier this month — good beach weather — but it sits at the front edge of typhoon season, which builds through September.

The two risks that actually bite here: landslides closing the Sapa road, which matters most on the Friday you drive down to catch the Da Nang flight; and a typhoon or tropical storm on the central coast, which can flatten the beach days and, at worst, disrupt a domestic flight. Both are visible in the forecast three or four days out.

Keep the Da Nang days loose, treat Fansipan as weather-dependent rather than fixed, and watch the Sapa road forecast ahead of the 28th. Windy or nchmf.gov.vn beat generic weather apps for the three-to-five day view.
